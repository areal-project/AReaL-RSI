## Astra L3P10: shared local progress without whole-task correctness

All four initially chose broadly the same Triton NHWC/tf32x3/fused-BatchNorm approach, before receiving leader advice. Initial convergence therefore cannot be blamed on sharing. Around minute 5 their compiled full candidates failed precision checks, with maximum errors around 2.8e-4–4.3e-4. GPU transport and compilation were working.

The leader often did useful technical checking: it separated convolution and BatchNorm errors, inspected training/evaluation semantics, and corrected its own channels-last warning after reading a hybrid script that already called `.contiguous()`. This is a positive example of raw-evidence access preventing an incorrect generalization.

Nevertheless, most of the hour became variations of the same numerical reconstruction problem: stem BatchNorm statistics/rounding and three layer2 convolutions relative to the reference FFT route. Different local probes did not create genuinely different end-to-end strategies. Around minute 55, R4 matched all 64 stem mean/inverse-standard-deviation values in a diagnostic; peers imported/shared this result. Whole-network checks still failed, around 1.55e-4–2.04e-4. Local exactness was useful evidence, not task success. All researchers continued; there was no instruction to freeze a good artifact halfway through the budget.

This supports a concrete concern about the leader's search policy: it kept organizing increasingly detailed work inside a shared numerical basin without obtaining an end-to-end feasible candidate. It does not yet prove that leader coordination caused the failure; the pure pass@4 control is needed because the initial basin was independently chosen. The later decision experiment should ask whether successive diagnostics change the feasibility of the main hypothesis, not merely whether they produce another local fact.

## Astra L2P71: sharing corrected real invalidity, at a large search cost

The positive chain is visible: R2 found range counterexamples, leader inspected raw results, other researchers withdrew invalid BF16/FP16 candidates, and protected arithmetic recovered much of the performance. Apparent score drops were often deliberate withdrawals, not a lost-best-result bug. Final search best 4.4928× remained eligible and passed the seed-137 final check.

Compared with Sol, Astra spent much more time investigating extreme finite FP32 values, overflow and denormals. Some counterexamples used values far outside the benchmark's ordinary random-input distribution. This is a difference in how thoroughly the team examined original semantics; formal random tests do not establish all-FP32 correctness. It would be misleading to call Sol's entire score advantage a better search policy, or to call every lower-precision solution cheating. No extra cross-run correctness test has been injected into either run. A common robustness audit, if needed later, must be separately identified and must not silently change the benchmark criterion.

## Sol L2P71: useful recombination, disappearing alternatives, missing correction

R1 initially explored direct FMA and Winograd, R4 direct CUDA, and R2/R3 Tensor Cores. R4's direct CUDA improved from about 1.00 to 0.944 ms, then a few variants regressed. Around minute 16 it read peer reports/candidates and switched to BF16. Leader had encouraged the CUDA route earlier; this switch was not simply a forced leader instruction. R1 abandoned one 2.2 ms Winograd implementation, later combined peer BF16 ideas with weight prepacking and row segmentation to reach about 0.336 ms. This was productive recombination, but the initially different algorithm families disappeared quickly. Later work largely tuned residual decomposition and tile layouts; similar final scores alone do not establish independent breakthroughs.

A specific actionable follow-up failure is recorded in the raw journals:

1. R4's public progress at wall minute 32.87 says the installed Triton supports only 1-D `tl.cat`, after three failed compilations.
2. At 14:50:56 UTC the leader advises R2 to try 2-D `tl.cat`, conditional on a colleague confirming support. The published failure already exists, although the formal report comes later.
3. At 14:53:55 the leader's own plan explicitly records that 2-D `tl.cat` is unsupported.
4. No correction to the earlier recipient appears in subsequent advice. At 15:01:10 R2 is still changing `can_reorder=True`; R3 has a similar attempt, but its causality is not established.

The information reached the leader; the broken step was delivering a correction to a researcher following stale guidance. This justifies adding explicit follow-up to the decision experiment, rather than another general request to report more often.

## Subsequent semantic range audit

After v0.2.6, the same operator-only finite-FP32 range probe was run on all eight final L2P71 artifacts from the first four batches. This batch’s Sol artifact, like v0.2.6 Sol, returns NaN for x=1e5 and weights=1e-6, while the reference remains finite. The unscaled FP16 decomposition has a value-range limitation. Official scores remain unchanged: this probe lies outside the public uniform [0,1) input sampler and was not part of the frozen scored protocol. The result limits claims of full FP32 semantic equivalence; it does not establish deliberate cheating. The other six artifacts pass this probe, and all eight pass the separate cancellation probe. No audit feedback was exposed to agents, and no reselection or timing repetition was performed. See ../semantic-audit-range/.
